THE RESEARCH PATH

How the questions evolved.

  1. September 15, 2026

    From flat weights to neuron structure

    Early adder studies compared flattened weights with simple parameter statistics. Controlled rewiring experiments led to a shared-neuron encoder whose prediction is unchanged by hidden-neuron renumbering. A frozen reader was then tested on 40 fresh target initializations.

    Evidence & details →
  2. October 15, 2026

    Reading a particular query

    Query-specific attention brought the question closer to individual answers. A matched regional-attention comparison improved probability estimates and exposed how difficult reliable localization remained.

    Evidence & details →
  3. October 2026

    Following learning over time

    The lifetime-reader work expanded the target population to 2,304 roots, two rules and eight training ages. The released activation-assisted Adder Lens reads target weights, a query and its hidden activations.

    Evidence & details →
  4. October 30, 2026

    Reading image classifiers

    MNIST experiments compared direct-weight and tensor-statistics readers. The compact statistics reader achieved lower development error than both the raw-weight reader and the constant-mean baseline.

    Evidence & details →
  5. October 30–31, 2026

    Building a richer visual testbed

    Redesigned custom datasets separated six visual tasks: sprites, shapes, textures, counting, location and direction. The gallery exposes the actual training images; ten trained target classifiers are now released.

    Evidence & details →
  6. November 16–17, 2026

    From reading to reconstruction

    Neuron completion predicts a missing neuron’s parameters. The public demo lets readers inspect weights and compare the intact, blanked and repaired networks, including their answer confidence.

    Evidence & details →

Regional attention improved probability estimates in a matched comparison. Query discrimination showed a smaller, uncertain change; paired-clone checks exposed remaining localization problems.

WHERE THIS IS HEADING

The next useful questions.

Research directions, with room to follow the evidence.

CURRENT FOCUS

Better reconstruction

Improve missing-neuron predictions and evaluate the repaired network against its intact baseline, including answer confidence and behavior.

CURRENT FOCUS

Richer image experiments

Use the redesigned custom visual tasks to investigate what readers can learn from image-network parameters and structure.

LONGER-TERM DIRECTION

Useful representations

Build representations of learned capabilities that help locate behavior, compare networks and guide training decisions.

This roadmap describes intended directions, not completed results or scheduled releases.
